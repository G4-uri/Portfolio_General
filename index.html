ERCOT tail-risk calibration: VaR for day-ahead and real-time collateral

A volatility-filtered, time-of-day Value-at-Risk for ERCOT supplier collateral, walk-forward tested on 31,119 real 5-minute steps at HB_HOUSTON and 4,275 causal day-ahead steps at DA_NORTH, with no look-ahead. It sizes collateral; it is not a price forecaster, and it does not place bids or route orders.

What it produces

For a 50 MW ERCOT position: a 99% Value-at-Risk sized by hour of day and current volatility (filtered historical simulation, EWMA volatility with λ = 0.94), scored side by side against a static parametric VaR and an empirical-by-hour VaR on the same walk-forward steps, so the gap between them is visible rather than assumed away.

A static VaR hides where it fails

A standard parametric VaR99 looks fine in aggregate — 0.83% breaches against a 1% target — but that is two errors cancelling: it breaches 2.31% of the time in the 17:00–22:00 evening ramp, where the largest losses land, while over-protecting the rest of the day. Its breaches also cluster (Christoffersen independence rejected, p < 0.001), and when it misses, the loss averages 2.65× the VaR it was supposed to cover.

Electricity prices are not equity prices

Hawkes and jump-diffusion models come from equity microstructure, where log-returns are standard. LMPs go to zero and below. Four years of real ERCOT DAM_NORTH data, including Winter Storm Uri, contain 18 non-positive hourly prices, and under a log-return formulation those silently produced NaN and −∞ parameters. I rebuilt the model in arithmetic price differences, as in Cartea & Figueroa (2005), and reworked the fair-value and haircut formulas around it.

A bug I found and withdrew

An earlier version of this work, including a conference abstract, reported the jump-diffusion fair value beating persistence by 2.6% in day-ahead MAE. That traced to a unit-scaling bug: the drift term multiplied a per-bar drift statistic by the horizon in seconds instead of bars, inflating it 3,600× for hourly bars, so it hit its clip (−5% of the last price) on almost every step and acted as a constant downward shift. Day-ahead price changes are skewed — the median step is −$0.22, the mean is −$0.003 — and MAE rewards the median, so the clipped drift was exploiting that skew, not predicting jumps. Corrected, the model posts $6.2504 MAE against persistence’s $6.2545: 0.1% better, not an edge. I’m publishing the correction alongside the withdrawal, not quietly dropping the number.

Hawkes clustering: tested and not supported

The original design used Hawkes self-excitation to raise risk estimates after a price spike. Auditing the estimator against synthetic data with a known, zero branching ratio found it reports 0.37 anyway — ten free parameters fit to a few hundred events — and that its reported ratio is actually double the true spectral radius, so an earlier result described as 0.60 was really 0.30. A corrected, pooled estimator does better, but testing it on 155 days of real HB_HOUSTON price episodes against a time-of-day-aware null found no clustering beyond time of day itself, with power to detect only about 55–62% of a true branching ratio of 0.3. The VaR results below don’t depend on this layer; it’s switched off.

What is not proven yet

  • One node and one season per horizon: HB_HOUSTON for real-time (summer to autumn), DA_NORTH for day-ahead (one winter half-year). The two are comparable in method, not in market conditions.
  • Only the 99% level is tested. Breach severity is reported but not targeted, and expected-shortfall haircuts are untested.
  • Breaches still cluster day-ahead (Christoffersen rejected for every method with breaches), independence is borderline for the real-time filtered VaR at λ = 0.94, and Kupiec p-values are overstated throughout.
  • Headline settings (2-hour buckets, λ = 0.94) were fixed before results. λ = 0.90 and the relative-loss day-ahead variant were examined afterwards and are exploratory.
  • Hawkes self-excitation is unsupported, not disproven: 155 real days show no clustering beyond time of day, but weak clustering near 0.3 is detected only 55–62% of the time, and memory outside 6 hours to 3 days is untested.
  • The loss is a one-sided drop on a fixed 50 MW position held one step. Buyer-side risk, multi-step holding, ERCOT’s actual collateral rules and day-ahead daylight-saving transitions are not modelled.

I would value 20 minutes on whether a filtered, time-of-day VaR is how a desk would actually size collateral, and whether you would want expected-shortfall or multi-step haircuts tested next.

Endogenous Order Flow Dynamics and Jump Risk in Smart Order Routing: A Hawkes Jump-Diffusion Cross-Asset Study of Crypto Microstructure

Gauri Santhosh Nair and Jinny Ann John, Department of Mathematics, CHRIST University. A bivariate Hawkes process and a Lee–Mykland jump detector are embedded in the Cont–Kukanov execution-cost model and validated on real Binance tick data across 26 trading days.

Order-flow modelBivariate Hawkes, exponential kernels, MLE (SLSQP), η < 0.90

Model selectionΔAIC = AIC(Poisson) − AIC(Hawkes), decisive above 10

Finding 1: order-flow clustering is universal

Hawkes beat the Poisson baseline in 253 of 259 windows (97.7%), with ΔAIC up to 2,607. The 6 exceptions are all XRP during the Dec 24–28 Christmas low-liquidity period, where event counts are too low for a 12-parameter model. I treat that as a limit of the model, not noise.
