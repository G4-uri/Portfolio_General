ERCOT LMP pricing oracle for supplier-side risk

A price and collateral-risk model for day-ahead bid sizing and collateral haircuts. It is not an execution or order-routing system, and it does not place bids or trades.

What it produces

For a given ERCOT settlement point: a fair-value LMP for the next interval, a confidence interval, and a collateral haircut that shows a jump-diffusion Value-at-Risk next to a separate Extreme Value Theory (EVT) tail estimate, so neither silently overrides the other.

Electricity prices are not equity prices

Hawkes and jump-diffusion models come from equity microstructure, where log-returns are standard. LMPs go to zero and below. Four years of real ERCOT DAM_NORTH data, including Winter Storm Uri, contain 18 non-positive hourly prices, and under a log-return formulation those silently produced NaN and −∞ parameters. I rebuilt the model in arithmetic price differences, as in Cartea & Figueroa (2005), and reworked the fair-value and haircut formulas around it.

A bug I found in my own result

An earlier run showed an edge that traced back to two bugs: a unit-scaling error in the drift, and a data-loading defect that replaced real price spikes, Uri’s included, with interpolated values. After fixing both, the edge reproduced at 2.6%. I report it here because I verified it, not because it looked good.

Why EVT alongside Hawkes

The Hawkes fit needs roughly 45 to 90+ days of 5-minute history to identify about ten parameters, and my real pulls have not reached that. I confirmed it directly: 0 valid windows. So I added a Peaks-Over-Threshold model with a 2-parameter Generalized Pareto tail, which is stable at current sample sizes and standard for electricity spikes.

What is not proven yet

  • Hawkes self-excitation has not calibrated on real ERCOT data at any resolution I have tested. Jump-diffusion and EVT carry the real value today.
  • “Beats naive persistence” is a deliberately weak baseline. It shows the pipeline is sound, not that it competes with a desk forecast or vendor model.
  • Validation covers two settlement points (DA_NORTH and HB_HOUSTON). Other nodes are untested.
  • Several parameters are reasoned defaults, not fitted values: the Hawkes decay range, the SCED-RTD trust weight, and the EIA-shock threshold.
  • The Bitcoin-mining demand proxy is the weakest signal in the stack and is flagged as such.

I would value 20 minutes on whether the DAM–RTM basis framing is how a desk would actually use this, and which dataset or benchmark forecast you would trust more than naive persistence.

Endogenous Order Flow Dynamics and Jump Risk in Smart Order Routing: A Hawkes Jump-Diffusion Cross-Asset Study of Crypto Microstructure

Gauri Santhosh Nair and Dr Jinny Ann John, Department of Mathematics, CHRIST University. A bivariate Hawkes process and a Lee–Mykland jump detector are embedded in the Cont–Kukanov execution-cost model and validated on real Binance tick data.
